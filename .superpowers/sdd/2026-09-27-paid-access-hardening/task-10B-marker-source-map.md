## Historical writer verified at feature base

Read-only `git show` of backend feature base `473a39bee299f3df4c7fdcf6f45502f880a9bd8b` confirms an additional historical automatic writer: `DeviceContextService.ResolveAsync` called `entitlements.FindForDeviceAsync(device.DeviceUuid)`, then called `MarkClaimedPurchaseAsync(keyId)` whenever the selected subscription had non-null `ClaimedAt`. If the subscription later disappeared and the marker remained without account, it projected `account_required`. The current anonymous resolver removed that UUID-based selection and automatic writer.

The historical account claim service also set the exact key's marker after successful `TryClaimAsync`. These two paths explain why marker provenance cannot establish current purchase ownership. In particular, the old UUID-based selection could mark a different key sharing a historical library UUID; treat that as a documented possible false positive and recoverable restriction, never an inferred owner or `WasEverPaid=true`. This is source-supported provenance, not a production inventory or assertion that such rows actually exist in production.
